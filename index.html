Research objective

Go beyond traditional cricket statistics and identify players who repeatedly deliver valuable performances under difficult match situations.

Our central principle

The best pressure performer is not necessarily the player with the highest runs or wickets. Match situation, contribution, role and repeatability matter.

Our analytical model

The framework combines contribution, repeatability and match context. It does not claim that an unvalidated weighted sum is a scientific score.

Batting formulas

Strike Rate = (Runs / Balls Faced) × 100

20+ Rate = (20+ Scores / Matches) × 100

Required Run Rate = Runs Required / Overs Remaining

Wides are excluded from balls faced. Role and chase context are assessed separately.

Bowling formulas

Economy = Charged Runs / (Legal Balls / 6)

Wicket Rate = (Credited Wickets / Legal Balls) × 24

Runs Saved = Expected Runs − Actual Charged Runs

Runs Saved per 24 = (Total Runs Saved / Legal Balls) × 24

Byes and leg-byes are excluded from bowler-charged runs. Non-bowler-credit dismissals are not counted as bowler wickets.

How the model works

  1. Collect and audit IPL match and delivery records.
  2. Calculate player-level batting and bowling indicators.
  3. Identify match situations where pressure is meaningful.
  4. Assess repeatability, role and workload.
  5. Validate expected-runs and win-probability models.
  6. Compare evidence and select the final rankings.

Interpretation

  • The expected-runs model showed relatively small average bias.
  • The win-probability model showed useful match-outcome discrimination.
  • Bowling run-prevention performance showed positive repeatability across qualifying players.
  • The wicket model had modest predictive power and was not used to assign highly precise wicket values.
  • Pressure proxies were not sufficiently validated to become one final numerical score.

Important limitations

  • Four matches did not have ball-by-ball coverage.
  • Some extra-run classifications were ambiguous.
  • The expected-runs model had a small positive bias.
  • The 2023–2026 bowling repeatability comparison did not include 2022.
  • Some pressure proxies were not sufficiently validated for a unified score.
  • Final positions are reasoned selections, not statistically proven exact differences.

Pressure performance is not simply about how much a player produces. It is about how consistently and effectively the player contributes when the match situation demands it.
